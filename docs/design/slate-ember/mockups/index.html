Slate & Ember

Warm graphite instead of a blue-cast One Dark, content on raised islands, a declared 16px base, and one state rail as the only accent gesture. The four screens below are the shipped design; ../README.md is why each decision is the one it is.

What was wrong, measured

Three complaints, and none of them was a matter of taste. Every number below came out of the stylesheet this replaces.

Nothing grouped

--border measured 1.28:1 against its own panel — a border nobody can see. There was no raised surface in the file at all, so the only hierarchy left was type size, and the type was small.

Empty where it needed density

minmax(240px, 1fr) spread four board columns across the whole window while .pr-detail-body capped text at 72ch in the middle of 1970px. The sidebar was one undifferentiated scroll of headings and rows.

Five hues for four meanings

A blue accent competed with green, amber, cyan and red states — five hues arguing on a screen whose whole job is telling you what a dozen sessions are doing.

Three decisions; everything else follows

01 · islands

Content lives on raised, rounded, hairlined surfaces above a darker ground. Elevation is three things at once — a hairline, an inner top highlight, a soft shadow; any one of them alone is a flat box again.

02 · hue = state

The accent is near-achromatic light. In an app whose job is reporting the state of N concurrent sessions, hue is a scarce resource and it belongs to state. Green, amber and red mean working, needs-you and broken, and nothing else ever borrows them. Ended has no hue: it is the absence of a signal, not a signal.

03 · the rail

The one flourish: a 3px bar on the left edge of every tile, row and card, carrying its state colour. A dozen tiles read in one sweep instead of by reading a dozen small chips. One gesture for the whole interface; three would be noise.

Palette

Authored in OKLch and resolved to sRGB by ../tools/palette.mjs, which also measures every pair the design asserts. Hex and rgba() in the stylesheet because scripts/contrast.mjs parses those and nothing else — a colour that script cannot read is a colour nobody can check.
